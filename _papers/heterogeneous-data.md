<p><em>The starting question: can one deep-learning pipeline make sense of emergency records where every case looks structurally different?</em></p>
<p><strong>The problem.</strong> A single emergency record can contain numbers, categories, timestamps, and text, recorded by different people to different standards, with large parts absent. Classical models expect tidy, uniform inputs and struggle with this kind of variety.</p>
<p><strong>What I did.</strong> As part of a multi-author chapter from the Organic Computing programme, I contributed to a deep-learning pipeline that ingests this heterogeneous data and classifies several targets at once &mdash; diagnostic categories, medications, and physiological measurements &mdash; so that a TeleNotArzt receives structured suggestions faster.</p>
<p><strong>Why it matters.</strong> This was the proof of concept: it showed the heterogeneity of emergency data is workable, and it set up the more focused papers on data quality, synthetic data, and the full assistant system.</p>
<p><strong>In short:</strong> the first step &mdash; one pipeline that classifies diagnoses, medications, and measurements straight from messy emergency records.</p>
