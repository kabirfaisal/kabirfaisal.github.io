Research profile

Research & academic interests

Building a recommendation system for diagnosis support in emergency medicine — robust in complex, heterogeneous, and time-critical healthcare environments.

The main focus of my PhD at Christian-Albrechts-Universität zu Kiel is developing a recommendation system for diagnosis support in emergency medicine — a system that helps clinicians reach a diagnosis from incomplete, heterogeneous, and time-critical patient data. Around that goal I work on AI-supported integration of multimodal clinical data (text, image, tabular, and time-series), missing-value imputation, synthetic data generation, and the design and evaluation of the underlying deep-learning models. This work is grounded in the Intelligent Systems Group at Kiel University and in the KIT2 (TeleNotArzt) project — a completed real-world collaboration with RWTH Aachen University.

Current research focus

  • A recommendation system for diagnosis support in emergency medicine (main PhD focus)
  • Deep learning for heterogeneous and multimodal medical data
  • Missing value imputation for medical time-series and tabular data
  • Synthetic data generation for healthcare applications
  • Disease classification and treatment recommendation in urgent care

Research strengths

  • Design and evaluation of AI models for medical emergencies
  • Preprocessing and imputation of incomplete clinical datasets
  • Comparative analysis of deep learning and tabular generative models
  • Multimodal learning across time-series, tabular, and text inputs
  • Translating analytical results into decision-support tools

Research themes

Diagnosis Recommendation Systems Emergency Medicine AI Clinical Decision Support Missing Value Imputation Deep Learning Generative AI Synthetic Data Multimodal Learning Healthcare Analytics

Research approach

I approach research through rigorous modeling, comparative evaluation, and practical relevance to real-world healthcare problems. The goal is a diagnosis recommendation system that is reliable under the conditions of emergency care — missing values, mixed data types, and decisions made under time pressure — by combining advanced machine-learning methods with a domain-specific understanding of clinical data quality and uncertainty.
