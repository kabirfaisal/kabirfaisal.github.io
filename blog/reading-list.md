<h2>Reading list</h2>
<p class="muted">The references I point people to when they want the full version of the explainers above.</p>
</div>
<div class="grid cols-2">
<article class="card card-hover" data-reveal>
<h3>Books</h3>
<ul class="tick-list">
<li><strong>Deep Learning</strong> &mdash; Goodfellow, Bengio &amp; Courville. The standard reference; free at <a href="https://www.deeplearningbook.org" target="_blank" rel="noopener">deeplearningbook.org</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Understanding Deep Learning</strong> &mdash; Simon Prince. Modern, visual; free PDF at <a href="https://udlbook.github.io/udlbook/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">udlbook.github.io</a>.</li>
<li><strong>The Elements of Statistical Learning</strong> &mdash; Hastie, Tibshirani &amp; Friedman. Trees, boosting, the statistical view; free PDF.</li>
<li><strong>Pattern Recognition and Machine Learning</strong> &mdash; Bishop. The probabilistic foundations.</li>
</ul>
</article>
<article class="card card-hover" data-reveal>
<h3>Courses &amp; sites</h3>
<ul class="tick-list">
<li><strong>Dive into Deep Learning</strong> &mdash; interactive book with runnable code, <a href="https://d2l.ai" target="_blank" rel="noopener">d2l.ai</a>.</li>
<li><strong>fast.ai</strong> &mdash; practical deep learning, top-down.</li>
<li><strong>Distill.pub</strong> &mdash; visual explanations of attention, momentum, and more.</li>
<li><strong>The Illustrated Transformer</strong> &mdash; Jay Alammar&rsquo;s walk-through of attention.</li>
</ul>
</article>
</div>
